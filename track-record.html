Why week by week

A single day's record is mostly noise — a handful of picks either hit or they don't, and that says more about that day's games than about the model. Weekly buckets are large enough to smooth that out while still showing a real trend, not a season-to-date average that takes months to move. Weeks with no graded picks in a market show as a gap in that line rather than a false 0%.

Why HR Threats gets its own chart

HR Threats predicts one specific batter homering in one specific game — a realistic range is roughly 10-20%, not 50/50. Plotted on the same 0-100% axis as Game Picks and Strikeout Props above, it would always look like a flat line pinned near the bottom no matter how well it's actually doing, so it gets its own appropriately-scaled chart here instead.

Every HR Threats pick carries a real predicted probability the moment it's captured — summed across a day's graded picks, that's an honest "expected home run count" the model itself generated. This chart plots that expected count against how many of those same picks actually went deep, over the last 45 days. If Projected consistently runs above Actual, the model is overconfident; below, underconfident. This tracks Diamond Report's own picks specifically, not every home run hit across MLB that day.

Performance Breakdown

Every loss isn't graded the same way here — a game decided by one run, a strikeout total that landed within one K of the line, or a real warning-track fly out that stayed in the park all get separated from the rest as a near miss, not lumped in with an outright miss. Near misses still count as a loss in the Hit Rate figures below — they're broken out separately so you can see how close a pick came, not treated as a partial win. Pick a window below to see hits/near-misses/misses by market, by confidence level, by team, and which players had the highest real hit rate.

"Near miss" definitions — Game Picks: lost a one-run game. Strikeout Props: final K total within 1 strikeout of the line. HR Threats: no home run, but a real warning-track fly out (≥375ft — the same distance the "🚀 NEAR HR" chip uses) that same game. Confidence/team/leaderboard splits only show a real percentage once a market has at least 20 graded picks in that split (8 for an individual player on the leaderboard) — smaller samples are shown but marked as low-sample rather than hidden, since a single-digit sample can swing wildly and isn't a reliable signal either way.
